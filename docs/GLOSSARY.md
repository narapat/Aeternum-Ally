# Glossary

Domain and technical terms used in Aeternum Ally. Intended for developers new to sustainability reporting or newcomers to the codebase.

---

## Sustainability standards & regulations

**CSRD — Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive**
European Union law (effective 2024 onwards, phased by company size) that mandates large companies and eventually many SMEs to publish annual sustainability reports. ESRS defines what those reports must contain.

**ESRS — European Sustainability Reporting Standards**
The technical standards that specify what to disclose under CSRD. Divided into:
- ESRS 1: General requirements (how to apply the standards)
- ESRS 2: General disclosures (governance, strategy, materiality process)
- Topical standards: E1–E5 (Environmental), S1–S4 (Social), G1 (Governance)

**GRI — Global Reporting Initiative**
An older, widely adopted sustainability reporting framework. Predates ESRS. Many companies already report to GRI. ESRS topics map to GRI standards, so a GRI Content Index in the Sustainability Statement lets users satisfy both at once.

**ISIC — International Standard Industrial Classification**
UN system for classifying economic activities into codes and categories. Used in the Company Profile to standardize industry identification across countries.

## Core concepts

**Double Materiality**
The defining methodology of ESRS. A topic is material if it is significant on either of two dimensions:
1. **Impact Materiality (Inside-Out):** The company's impact on people and the planet — negative or positive, actual or potential.
2. **Financial Materiality (Outside-In):** How the sustainability topic affects the company's financial performance through risks or opportunities.
A topic only needs to exceed the threshold on one dimension to be considered material.

**IRO — Impact, Risk, Opportunity**
The three types of findings produced by a Double Materiality Assessment for a given topic:
- **Impact:** How the company affects the world (positive or negative)
- **Risk:** How external sustainability conditions could harm the company financially
- **Opportunity:** How sustainability trends could benefit the company financially

**Material topic**
An ESRS topic whose calculated materiality score exceeds the threshold (currently 40 out of 100) on either the Impact or Financial dimension. Material topics require dedicated disclosure in the Sustainability Statement.

**Materiality threshold**
The numerical cut-off separating material from non-material topics. In this codebase it is `MATERIALITY_THRESHOLD = 40` in `constants.ts`. Topics scoring above 40 on either axis appear in the upper-right of the Materiality Matrix and in the Material Topics List.

**Sustainability Statement**
The formal disclosure document required under CSRD/ESRS. Generated by the platform, it follows the ESRS 2 structure and covers all material topics. It is a draft — it should be reviewed and signed off by qualified persons before submission.

## Frameworks used in the product

**Business Model Canvas (BMC)**
A one-page strategic framework invented by Osterwalder & Pigneur. Describes how a company creates, delivers, and captures value across nine blocks: Key Partners, Key Activities, Key Resources, Value Proposition, Customer Relationships, Channels, Customer Segments, Cost Structure, Revenue Streams. Aeternum Ally extends this with two sustainability blocks: Eco-Social Costs and Eco-Social Benefits.

**Sustainable Business Model Canvas**
The extended BMC used in this product. The additional blocks surface the company's environmental and social externalities, which then seed the materiality assessment.

**Balanced Scorecard (BSC)**
A performance management framework by Kaplan & Norton. Organizes KPIs across four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Processes, Learning & Growth. Used in the Performance Dashboard to connect sustainability targets to business outcomes.

**SWOT Analysis**
Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. A standard strategic planning tool. In this product, Opportunities and Threats are augmented with AI-powered Google Search grounding to surface real-world sustainability-relevant market data.

**RACI**
Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. A matrix for assigning ownership of tasks or KPIs:
- **Responsible:** Does the work
- **Accountable:** Signs off and owns the outcome (one person only)
- **Consulted:** Provides input before decisions are made
- **Informed:** Notified of outcomes but not involved in decisions

## Technical terms (project-specific)

**Org / Organization**
The top-level tenant in the multi-tenant system. One organization = one company's data. All sustainability data is scoped to an org via `organization_id` foreign keys.

**OrgRole**
The four roles a member can hold within an organization: `Owner`, `Admin`, `Manager`, `Consultant`. Controls what data a user can read and write.

**RLS — Row-Level Security**
A Postgres feature that attaches access policies directly to database tables. In this codebase, RLS enforces that users can only see and modify data belonging to their own organization. RLS runs server-side at the database layer — it cannot be bypassed by application code.

**Singleton table**
A database table designed to hold exactly one row per organization (e.g. `company_profiles`, `business_model_canvases`, `swot_analyses`). These are auto-saved via the `useOrgData` hook.

**Netlify Functions**
Serverless functions deployed alongside the frontend on Netlify. In this project they handle operations that require the Supabase service-role key or the Gemini API key — secrets that must never reach the browser.

**Magic link**
A one-time sign-in URL sent by email. Clicking it authenticates the user without a password. Supabase Auth manages magic-link generation and delivery.

**Invite token**
A UUID that serves as both the primary key of the `organization_invites` row and the secret in the invitation email link. It is single-use: the `accept-invite` function deletes it after the invitee joins.

**anon key**
The Supabase anonymous API key. It is safe to include in the browser bundle because RLS prevents it from being used to access data outside the authenticated user's org. Contrast with the service-role key, which bypasses RLS and must never be sent to the browser.
